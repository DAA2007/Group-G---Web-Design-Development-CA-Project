Facts

Sustainale Development Goals 6

Access to clean water and safe sanitation is one of the most basic human needs for health and well‑being. Sustainable Development Goal 6 (SDG 6), adopted by the United Nations in 2015, aims to ensure the availability and sustainable management of water and sanitation for all by 2030. Around the world, billions of people still lack safely managed drinking water and sanitation services, which poses serious risks to health, deepens poverty, and threatens ecosystems.
Water is vital not only for drinking and hygiene but also for agriculture, industry, and sustaining the environment. Achieving SDG 6 requires investments in infrastructure, education in hygiene practices, and protection of water‑related ecosystems so that everyone can enjoy safe, affordable, and reliable access to this essential resource.

Global statics

Approximately one in four people worldwide, or 2.2 billion people, lack access to safely managed drinking water, with the majority living in rural areas, least developed countries, and fragile contexts. This equates to about 26% of the global population.

Sanitation Breakdown
Sanitation Access

3.4 billion people worldwide lack access to safely managed sanitation services.

Open Defecation

A sub-group of those without safely managed services, 354 million people, still practice open defecation.

Basic Hygiene

1.7 billion people lack basic hygiene services at home, including 611 million people with no facilities at all.

Local Context
  • Communities often rely on groundwater and local water supplies, which can be affected by contamination from agriculture, septic tanks, or industrial activity.
  • Seasonal shortages are common in many areas, especially during summer or drought periods.
  • Local authorities and governments monitor water quality and issue notices (like boil water advisories) when contamination is detected.
  • Wastewater treatment facilities are essential to protect rivers and lakes, but smaller towns and rural areas sometimes face challenges in maintaining them.
  • - Awareness campaigns and community projects (like rainwater harvesting or hygiene education) play a key role in improving sanitation and water access.
